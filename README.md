# Sales and Delivery Analysis Project

## Overview

In this project, I analyzed a supply chain dataset to better understand sales performance, product trends, and shipping efficiency. The goal was to find useful insights that could help a business improve operations.

**Note:** 

This analysis uses data from 2015–2017, with January 2018 included for overall analysis but excluded from monthly trend comparisons to maintain consistency.

**Dataset used:**

https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/shashwatwork/dataco-smart-supply-chain-for-big-data-analysis/data

## Tools Used

Excel → data cleaning and preparation

SQL → data analysis

Tableau → data visualization and dashboard

## Business Questions

I focused on answering a few key questions:

* What states generate the most sales?

* Which product categories bring in the most revenue?

* Which categories are the most profitable?

* How often are orders delivered late?

* Which shipping method struggles the most with delays?

* Are delivery timelines accurate?

* What do monthly sales trends look like?


## Key Insights

**Sales by State:**
 
Puerto Rico generates the highest sales at $12.7M, showing a heavy concentration of revenue in one region. The next highest state, California, brings in $5.3M, which highlights a significant gap and potential over-reliance on a single market.

**Top Revenue Category:**

The Fishing category brings in the most revenue at $6.2M, making it a major driver of overall sales performance.

**Most Profitable Category:**

Fishing is also the most profitable category. The ranking of top categories by profit closely matches the revenue rankings, suggesting that the highest-selling products are also the most efficient at generating profit.

**Late Deliveries:**

Approximately 57.33% of orders are delivered late, pointing to a clear issue within the shipping process that could impact customer satisfaction.

**Shipping Method Performance:**

First Class shipping has the highest late delivery rate at 95.27%, which is especially concerning since customers are paying for faster delivery.

Other shipping methods also show delays:

* Second Class: 76.72% late

* Same Day: 46.15% late

* Standard Class: 38.13% late (lowest, but still high)

Overall, delays are present across all shipping methods, not just one.

**Delivery Accuracy:**

Delivery performance shows that only 18.66% of orders arrive on time, while 57.33% arrive late and 24.01% arrive early.
This suggests that estimated delivery timelines are not very accurate and may need to be adjusted to better reflect actual performance.

**Monthly Trends:**

Monthly sales trends (2015–2017) remained relatively consistent overall, but there is a noticeable drop in revenue during November and December of 2017. This is unusual given the typical increase in sales during the holiday season and may point to external factors or data inconsistencies.
(Note: January 2018 data was excluded from this analysis to maintain consistency across full-year comparisons.)

## Recommendations
Based on the findings, here are a few areas the business could focus on:

* Improve shipping performance, especially for First Class delivery, to better meet customer expectations
  
* Reevaluate delivery time estimates so they more accurately reflect actual shipping performance
  
* Investigate the heavy reliance on Puerto Rico for revenue and explore opportunities to grow other regions
 
* Continue investing in high-performing categories like Fishing while identifying ways to improve lower-performing categories
  
* Look into the drop in sales during late 2017 to determine if it was caused by operational issues, seasonality, or missing data
